# Likely judge questions and suggested answers

These answers describe the published proof of concept. They are preparation material, not completed member reviews. Keep the map's Unknown fields and all stated limitations visible.

| Question | Suggested answer |
|---|---|
| What decision does this support? | It helps planners choose locations for further heat-protection investigation. It combines relative satellite indicators with local context; it does not approve an intervention or measure human heat risk. |
| Where is the AI? | ESA WorldCover is an upstream machine-learning-derived land-cover product. Our own pipeline uses deterministic QA, aggregation and ranking. We have not implemented our own VHR building segmentation or a predictive health model. |
| Does 50.4°C mean people breathe air at that temperature? | No. It is the mean of two date-level surface-temperature medians for a 300 m cell. It is neither air temperature nor a stop-level or personal-exposure measurement. |
| Why use 300 m cells when delivered Landsat pixels are 30 m? | Native thermal detail is about 100 m, and delivered samples are spatially dependent. Aggregating screened support avoids implying fine property-level temperature precision. It still cannot isolate a bus stop or individual tree. |
| Did you visit the sites? What did you measure? | Nasser confirms visits and the reported physical situation; exact visit dates were not supplied. Abdulrahman's qualitative observations are attributed. User counts, use times and detailed shade assessment were not measured. Visits do not constitute independent thermal validation. |
| Why does the map still say Unknown? | No evidence establishes those particular use, ownership or planned-use details. Confirming a visit does not fill those gaps. We preserve Unknown until a documented observation or measurement supports a change. |
| How can the bus-stop cell have zero greenery when there are trees? | Zero is the share detected under NDVI ≥0.30 on matched clear-land pixels, not a tree inventory. The pixel audit reproduces zero at that threshold, with lower-threshold signal present. Sparse or small trees can be poorly represented in the cell-scale signal. |
| Why only two temperature dates? | The 14 and 30 September 2026 scenes pass the coverage and uncertainty checks. September sixth fails: none of the historical urban inland support passed the strict QA limit. Two accepted snapshots cannot establish a seasonal or long-term trend. |
| How do you handle clouds and the coast? | QA screens cloud flags, uncertainty above 2 K, water and uncertain land; it also excludes a 60 m coastal buffer and crop-edge halo. Historical water, wetland and mangrove classes are conservatively excluded. Compare the common inland footprints retained on both dates. |
| Is the study boundary the whole official city? | No. The 51.445–51.555°E, 25.635–25.730°N window is analyst-defined and includes urban, desert and coastal context. Official-boundary and stakeholder validation remain pending. |
| How is priority calculated? | It combines relative ranks: heat 50%, low green signal 30%, mapped buildings 20%. Urban candidates require historical built-up coverage ≥10% or mapped buildings ≥2%. These choices can miss recent or unmapped development. |
| What do the thirteen scenarios prove? | They expose sensitivity to weights and screening settings. Rank ranges are not confidence intervals and do not establish a uniquely best intervention. The highlighted sites remain investigation candidates. |
| Are your reference checks an accuracy score? | No. Green reference checks pass 24 of 25, building checks 0 of 30, and all 30 coastal references are excluded from retained comparison. Contributed reference labels and shoreline context can conflict. These diagnostic results do not establish overall accuracy. |
| Would planting trees definitely cool these locations? | We did not estimate causal cooling or design interventions. Shade, shelters, trees and safer routes need site measurements, ownership/access checks, engineering assessment and stakeholder review. |
| What is reproducible? | The package contains 35 hashed inputs, metadata, code, executed notebook, figures and seven original tables. The previous expanded run passed five code cells without errors and reproduced the seven tables byte for byte. We did not rerun analysis for delivery. |
| Will the demo work without internet? | The standalone HTML embeds CSS, JavaScript, JSON data and twelve existing map images; galleries are ready but no site photographs were received. The map was technically tested in offline Chromium on desktop and a simulated phone; external Street View/source links need internet. ChatGPT's embedded preview and physical phones were not verified. |
| Who reviewed the project? | Abdulrahman's observations are recorded and Nasser confirmed visits. Automated technical checks are recorded separately. Mohammed's presentation practice, Abdulrahman's project review/photo collection, Majed's slide review and Ali's rehearsal remain pending until each member confirms completion. |
| What data can others reuse? | Code is MIT; source data retain their own licences and credits, including Copernicus, USGS, WorldCover CC BY 4.0 and OpenStreetMap ODbL. Refer to DATA-LICENSES.md. Publishing the repository is separate from hackathon submission. |
| What is the next step? | Measure site use and shade, verify access/ownership and boundaries, seek planner feedback and independent validation, and extend dates only with suitable data. A longer-term study and our own segmentation remain future work. |

If a judge asks for an unmeasured number, say: "We have not measured that yet; it remains Unknown in the review. Here is the site check we would run next." Do not estimate passenger counts, waiting time, shade coverage, intervention cooling or health impact from the satellite ranking.

## Questions about the improvement revision

- Why choose V18-26 at rank 275? It illustrates reported jogging use and a shade inspection question. The deck's cases are team-selected examples, not a revised satellite shortlist. Hospital land remains in the complete results. Its mean date-median temperature is 47.47°C, retained coverage 72%, and scenario ranks 203–336.
- Did you implement historical land-change analysis? We prepared a separate Dynamic World workflow, but Earth Engine access is blocked and no local coverage/confidence or change result is verified. We do not describe it as a completed historical comparison. 2023 is deferred.
- Why might nearby mangroves coexist with low greenery? Their current geometry, the whole route and the reported viewing direction are not documented. Historical data inspection does not establish the cause of the low retained-pixel NDVI signal. We keep the original value and do not infer there are no trees.
- What photos were collected? None have been received for this revision. The gallery shows Evidence pending; a synthetic software-test fixture is never shown as site evidence or included in the package.
- Does the unused-land report change V24-07's rank? No: it remains 4/373. The report is qualitative and its date/time is unspecified, so use at other times remains Unknown.
- How does this compare with earthobservation.land? Its specified public site could not be retrieved in this session and the domain search returned no results. We have not verified its feature set and claim no superiority, missing features or hackathon participation.
