# UrbanHeat AI: implemented PoC

UrbanHeat AI provides an explainable starting point for urban-planning site visits across the Al Khor area, Qatar. The broad analyst-defined window includes surrounding desert and coast and is not an official city boundary.

We combine two quality-screened September 2026 morning Landsat surface-temperature observations, Sentinel-2 green-pixel indicators, contributed OSM building footprints and historical WorldCover land cover. Conservative coastal and wetland exclusions and a common observation footprint support comparison. The map separates temperature, greenery, mapped buildings, relative investigation priority and uncertainty. It adds a separate local-review stage for five attributed local-report locations, with reported use, shade/access unknowns, comparison and a downloadable review table.

The analysis reports 373 screened urban candidate cells with 31.82 km² of retained sample footprint; 1111 observed cells remain inspectable. Thirteen alternative settings show that the leading candidates are sensitive to thresholds and weights. Contributed-reference checks have mixed results and human confirmation is pending. No overall accuracy or thermal calibration is claimed.

Abdulrahman supplied attributed observations identifying a bus-stop road, event-dependent stadium parking, hospital-associated empty land, a jogging route and unused land near a bus hub. The presentation uses the jogging route instead of hospital land as its third example; the complete results and original satellite top three are preserved. The V04-23 example combines cell-level heat screening with an inspection question about waiting-time shade and walking access. A March 2023 Street View reference is linked; Nasser confirms visiting the original three sites and the same described physical situation as in September 2026, with exact visit dates unspecified. A separate pixel audit reproduces the low greenery result without changing the tested threshold or ranking. No bus-stop temperature or pedestrian exposure measurement is claimed.

This is a first-stage open-data implementation of the submitted idea. WorldCover provides an upstream ML-derived product. We do not run our own VHR segmentation, separate paved surfaces, quantify shade or estimate human heat-health risk, cooling benefits or long-term urban expansion. Those features require additional data and validation.

The package includes hashed source crops, exact metadata, a notebook, pinned dependencies, portable interactive map, results and presentation.
